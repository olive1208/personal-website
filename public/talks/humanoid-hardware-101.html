Part 1Overview

Hardware decides how fast humanoids spread: it sets the price, shapes what the AI can learn, and is still hard to make at scale

1

Cost decides who can buy

Hardware is nearly all of a humanoid's build cost. In an Optimus Gen 2, actuators and hands are about 85% of it. Buyers judge on payback, so list prices of $85–250k need to fall for wide use.

~85%of Optimus Gen 2 BOM in actuators and hands
2

Body and brain are one system

An AI model learns to control one body: its joint layout, motor strength, sensors and timing. Transfer to a new body is improving but still needs new data and tuning. The hardware choice shapes the data a company collects.

1 body= its own data and tuning
3

Making them at scale is unsolved

Volumes are tiny next to cars. Precision screws and reducers have limited capacity, hands wear out, and yield and reliability are still being learned on the line.

Hands are the least mature subsystem; durability and fine control will decide the winners

Exhibit 8Tendon-driven dexterous hand, cutaway

3Tendons · high-strength fiber cables
4Wrist joint
5Finger joints · 6–25 DoF per hand

Why it matters

Hands are where the robot does work. They are 17–19% of a full BOM; an Optimus hand pair costs about $9.5k.

Bottleneck

  • Durability: tendon wear, heat in tiny motors
  • No standard design: tendon, direct drive, linkage and hybrids coexist
  • Manipulation data lags the hardware

Who leads

Dexterous hand shipments by company Inspire Robots (China)~10,000 Linkerbot (China)10,000+ 1X (US, in-house)10,000 target

Inspire: 2025 deliveries. Linkerbot: total shipped by Dec 2025. 1X: 2026 target. Over 30,000 hands shipped industry-wide in 2025. Tesla and Figure build their own; Shadow (U.K.) serves research.

So what

A moat still being decided. Simple hands are already in a price war; durable, high-DoF hands are not.

Part 2Components · Compute and electronics

In compute, the onboard AI chip is the critical part and NVIDIA holds the moat; the rest of the electronics is commodity

Exhibit 10Robot brain module and a joint driver board

1SoC: Blackwell GPU + 14-core Arm CPU · 2,070 FP4 TFLOPS
2Memory · 128 GB LPDDR5X
3Power management · 40–130 W
4Connector to carrier board, cameras, network
5Microcontroller · runs the motor control loop
6Power transistors · drive the motor current

What is Jetson Thor?

Jetson AGX Thor is NVIDIA's robot computer module. Its main chip, Thor, puts a GPU of NVIDIA's Blackwell generation (the same design family as its data-center GPUs) next to a 14-core Arm CPU.

Brain module vs driver board

Brain module (1 per robot): sees, plans and runs the AI model. Driver board (1 per joint): turns the brain's commands into motor current thousands of times a second, like nerves to a muscle.

Who leads

  • NVIDIA Jetson Thor: default outside Tesla; used by Agility, Boston Dynamics, Figure, Unitree H2 Plus
  • Tesla: in-house AI chips reused from its cars
  • Driver boards and bus: TI, Infineon, NXP; EtherCAT, CAN FD

So what

Compute is a small share of cost (AI chip and cameras ~4% of an Optimus Gen 2; a G1's computing ~3%). The moat belongs to the chip vendor: NVIDIA raised the Thor kit from $3,499 to $5,499 in July 2026.

Part 4Open questions

Open question 1: cost is the gate to wide use; at $20–50k, a humanoid doing half a person's work pays back in about a year

Exhibit 14How far price and cost could fall, $ thousands

Levers

Scale (Goldman saw a ~40% drop in one year) · cheaper precision parts (roller screws now ¥499 vs ¥5,000+) · fewer, standard actuators · in-house actuator design

So what

At today's $150k list prices payback takes years. It gets to about a year only when price falls toward $50k and the robot does at least half a person's work.

Exhibit 15Payback in years, US factory, by robot price and output vs a person

How payback is calculated

  1. Labor saved a year = robot hours × output vs a person × labor cost. Example: 3,000 h × 50% × $36.70 = $55,050
  2. Upkeep a year = 15% of price (maintenance, software, parts). For a $50k robot: $7,500
  3. Payback = price ÷ (labor saved − upkeep) = $50,000 ÷ $47,550 = 1.1 years

Why $36.70 an hour: what US employers pay per hour, wages plus benefits, for production and material-moving workers, the jobs humanoids target (BLS). 3,000 hours: robot working hours a year = 2 shifts × 8 h × 250 days = 4,000 h, × 75% uptime (charging, faults, resets).

Note: payback is an illustrative model, excluding integration and financing costs.

For: why in-house is a moat

  • Controls cost: Unitree lists a G1 at $13,500
  • Faster design loops on actuators and hands
  • Hardware tuned to the AI model and its data

Against

  • Heavy capital and slower to scale
  • Specialists spread costs over every robot maker
  • Even Tesla still buys actuator assemblies

Why the US is building locally

  • FCC Covered List (Jul 28, 2026): new humanoids and mobile robots need FCC approval to be sold in the US. A robot only qualifies as US-made if it is built in the US and at least 65% of its component cost is US-made (rising to 75% in 2029). Otherwise it is "foreign-produced" and blocked
  • Tariffs: a Section 232 investigation into robotics and machinery imports could lead to new tariffs
  • Magnets: export licensing on rare earth magnets since Apr 2025 adds supply risk
